Google Business Profile Booking Links: Your Book Button Is a Lead Form

By Bryan Esguerra · 25 November 2026

Google Business Profile booking links now come with a switch: you can hide a third-party booking partner yourself, and turn it back on anytime. It looks like a small settings update. It is really about who owns the first step of your lead.

TLDR: Google now lets businesses choose which third-party booking and reservation links show on their Google Business Profile. You can hide a partner with a switch and show it again later. Hiding is not deleting: to remove a partner for good, Google still says to contact the provider. The lesson for Lead Marketing Automation (LMA): your booking button is a lead entry point. Own it, and send every booking into one CRM.

What changed with Google Business Profile booking links

Some booking links appear on your profile automatically, through third-party partners. On 23 November 2026, Lisa Landsman from Google shared on LinkedIn: "You now choose which booking links appear on Google." She called it the "exact same self-serve control" Google added for food delivery links a few weeks earlier, now for appointment bookings and dining reservations. Search Engine Roundtable reported it the same day.

Google's help page now shows how it works. Open your Business Profile and select Booking. "Next to the third-party provider you want to hide, turn the switch off and select Confirm." To show the provider again later, turn the switch back on.

One more detail from Google's help page on booking providers: when you hide a provider, "Existing customer bookings and reminders stay active, but new bookings stop."

One honest catch: hiding is not deleting

The same Google help page still says: "You cannot manually delete third-party booking or reservation providers directly from your Business Profile settings." To remove a partner for good, Google says to contact the provider through its opt-out link, support form or account portal. Providers are required to process opt-out requests within 5 business days. If they don't, you can report a third-party violation.

So the switch hides a partner from your profile. It does not end your account with that provider. And if you don't see a switch next to a partner you want gone, use the provider route.

Why your booking button is a lead entry point

A booking is not just a calendar slot. It is a person who raised their hand. For many local businesses, the Book button on Google is the first move a customer makes.

Now ask where that booking lands. If it goes to a partner app nobody checks, the follow-up never starts. You also lose the source, so you can't tell which bookings came from your Google profile. Google also notes that when you book through Google, "your provider might charge you."

That does not mean you should switch every partner off. Landsman makes the other side of the case: "Keeping a mix of trusted partners can still help you capture bookings from clients and guests who already have saved details or loyalty points on those apps." The goal is choice on purpose, not zero partners.
