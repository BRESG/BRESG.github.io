Google Ads Book Appointment Conversion Goal: A Long Call Is Not a Booking

By Bryan Esguerra · 27 November 2026

The Google Ads Book Appointment conversion goal decides what your account calls a booking, and Smart Bidding learns from that. So here is a question worth five minutes today: is a 60 second phone call a booked appointment? Your Google Ads might say yes.

TLDR: Google Ads rewrote its help page into "About the Book Appointment conversion goal" (reported 24 November 2026). It lists 3 ways to track bookings: partner bookings, bookings on your website, and calls from ads. For calls, any call at least as long as the minimum you set counts as a booked appointment. The goal is not used by a campaign unless it is an account default or you add it. The lesson for Lead Marketing Automation (LMA): Smart Bidding chases what you count, so count real bookings and check which ones became sales.

What Google changed on the help page

Google Ads used to have a help page called "About Partner bookings". It is now "About the Book Appointment conversion goal". Search Engine Roundtable and Search Engine Land both reported it on 24 November 2026.

To be clear, this is a rewritten help page, not a product launch. Search Engine Roundtable put it simply: "this is not a new document, it was just massively updated." Search Engine Land said the rewrite "doesn't establish that all three conversion tracking methods are newly introduced."

What did change is the guidance. Google now says: "The Book Appointment conversion goal helps you measure and optimize campaigns to drive scheduled appointments, reservations, and service bookings."

The 3 ways the Book Appointment conversion goal counts a booking

Google's help page says: "There are 3 main ways to track appointment conversions under the Book Appointment conversion goal."

1. Partner bookings

If you use a booking tool that works with Google, bookings made through that partner are tracked for you. Google says these conversions "are only sent to Google after the appointment is actually booked". No code on your site.

2. Website bookings

If people book on your own site, you use the Google tag. Google says: "You install the Google tag on your website and add an event snippet to your appointment confirmation page." Note the words: confirmation page. Not the form page. A person who opens your form has not booked yet.

3. Calls from ads

This is the one to read twice. Google says: "You can set a minimum call duration threshold (such as 60 seconds). Any call that lasts at least that long counts as a booked appointment conversion."

So a long call counts as a booking. But a long call can be a price question, a wrong number, or someone who says "I'll think about it". Search Engine Land made the same point: advertisers "should also distinguish between an actual confirmed appointment and a phone call that may not result in a booking".

Two details people miss

The goal is not on by default for every campaign. Google's note says the goal "isn't automatically applied to individual campaigns unless set as an account-default goal". So add it to the campaigns that should bid for bookings.

Healthcare is excluded. Google says: "Google Ads doesn't allow appointment booking links or goals for healthcare campaigns. While healthcare advertisers might find the appointment booking goal option in their account, they shouldn't select or use it."

Why what you count is what you get

Google's own pitch for the goal is to "Train Smart Bidding strategies" to "prioritize high-intent customers who are ready to schedule an appointment, rather than just clicking on your ad."

That works both ways. Smart Bidding learns from the conversions you give it. Count clicks and you get clicks. Count long calls and you get long calls. Count real bookings and you get more people who book.

How Lead Marketing Automation checks every booking

Google Ads can count a booking. It cannot tell you if that person showed up and paid. That part lives in your own system.

3. Measure which bookings became sales

My take

I was a Google Ads Specialist, a Google Tech-1 role via TTEC, and I have worked in paid media since 2016. I also hold the Google Ads Measurement certification. Bidding is only as smart as the conversions you feed it.

My view on this goal: use it, but don't trust a call timer to tell you who booked. If you book on your site, fire the tag on the confirmation page. If you take bookings by phone, treat a long call as a maybe until your CRM says it booked. And check that the goal is really in the campaigns you care about.
